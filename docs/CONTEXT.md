# Context: the NYC 9/11 Document Portal and the wider record

Research snapshot as of 2026-09-13. Every fact below is cited; where sources
disagreed or a figure wasn't stated, that's noted rather than guessed.

## 1. What the release contains, and what's promised next

The City launched **sept11documents.cityofnewyork.us** (also reachable at
`nyc.gov/sept11docs`) on **September 8, 2026**, releasing roughly **170,000
pages** of records about health, air quality, and the City's response to
9/11 — the first tranche of a much larger promised disclosure.
[NYC Mayor's Office](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/09/25-years-later--mamdani-administration-opens-city-s-9-11-records) ·
[amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/)

**Agencies represented at launch:** DEP, DORIS (City archives — see
Giuliani-era material below), DCAS, FDNY, DDC, DOB. **Promised "going
forward"** (no specific dates given for when each lands): NYPD, DOHMH, EDC.
[amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/)

**The core of the first drop is the "68 boxes"**: more than 20,000
documents from DEP, found in August 2025 after DEP told the court — under
pressure from a City Council–mandated DOI inquiry — that it had had the
records all along. [amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/) ·
[911 Health Watch](https://www.911healthwatch.org/effort-to-get-new-york-city-to-release-9-11-documents/)
The release also includes **Giuliani-administration records held by the
City archives (DORIS)**, a **WTC7 collection**, and material from "several
other city agencies." [911 Health Watch](https://www.911healthwatch.org/effort-to-get-new-york-city-to-release-9-11-documents/)

**Named documents surfaced so far:**
- **The "Harding Memo"** — sent to then–Deputy Mayor Robert Harding,
  October 2001. Cites a Law Department estimate of **~35,000 potential
  toxic-exposure plaintiffs**, ~10,000 of whom were expected to actually
  file claims, and lays out legislative options to cap the City's
  liability. It was not found in City files — **9/11 Health Watch located
  it for $20 in the papers of the late journalist Wayne Barrett at the
  Dolph Briscoe Center for American History, University of Texas at
  Austin**, after the Law Department told the court it did not exist.
  [911 Health Watch](https://www.911healthwatch.org/press/911-health-watch-obtains-harding-memo-disproving-law-departments-claim-that-it-does-not-exist/) ·
  [Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/nyc-releases-9-11-air-quality-records-harding-memo-478471) ·
  [NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/manhattan/news/2026/03/30/new-york-city-denies-records-after-old-9-11-memo-warned-of-potential-liability-)
- **The "Fuchs memo,"** February 28, 2002 — from Ester Fuchs, then a
  special adviser in the Mayor's Office, to Corporation Counsel Michael
  Cardozo, Health Commissioner Thomas Frieden, Deputy Mayor Marc Shaw, and
  press secretary Ed Skyler. Summarizes concerns raised by Rep. Jerrold
  Nadler and notes DOHMH had told residents to clean with wet mops/rags
  despite at least low-level asbestos in dust in many Lower Manhattan
  buildings. [Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/news/nation-world/sept11-air-quality-toxins-mamdani-release-documents-ground-zero-asbestos-giuliani-bloomberg-20260908.html) ·
  [amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/)
- **The May 16, 2002 "Clean Up Initiative" memo** — author/recipient not
  yet identified in coverage; references FEMA's Brad Gair and EPA's Kathy
  Callahan; contains the line "we would not use the word 'risk' in any
  letters as it would defeat the purpose of creating public confidence."
  [amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/)
- **October 6, 2001 internal memo from Associate Health Commissioner Kelly
  McKinney**, warning that "air quality [for asbestos] at those locations
  is not yet suitable for reoccupancy" — issued the same day Mayor Giuliani
  publicly declared the air safe. [The City Reporter](https://www.thecityreporter.nyc/2026/09/08/mamdani-9-11-air-quality-documents-giuliani-bloomberg-eric-adams/)

**Additional records not yet in the portal:** coverage of the amNewYork
piece notes the City had, "within several months" prior, also received
**"millions of additional documents" held by the World Trade Center Captive
Insurance Company** — a distinct, much larger body of records whose release
timeline is unstated. [amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/)
Treat this figure cautiously — no other outlet corroborates the word
"millions" and no page count or format is given.

**Settlement (the FOIL cadence):** Two Article 78 FOIL proceedings brought
by 9/11 Health Watch — one against DEP, one against the Mayor's Office —
were resolved before NYS Supreme Court Justice James G. Clynes, who had
already ruled (May 28, 2026) that the City (under then-Mayor Eric Adams)
violated FOIL by withholding the files. The settlement requires **monthly
meetings for one year** between 9/11 Health Watch/counsel and City
officials to review documents produced, page counts, source agency, and
subject matter, and to agree additional search terms; Clynes retains
jurisdiction for that year and either side can reopen litigation over
disputes. [911 Health Watch settlement statement](https://www.911healthwatch.org/press/statement-on-historic-settlement-with-nyc-of-911-health-watchs-freedom-of-information-request-for-the-release-of-9-11-records/) ·
[amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/)

**Funding:** FY27 city budget carries **$34–34.2M** to build/maintain the
portal and fund the ongoing document review, plus a separate **$4M** for
DOI (below). [NYC Mayor's Office](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/09/25-years-later--mamdani-administration-opens-city-s-9-11-records) ·
[amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/)

**Future drops:** the City says the portal will be "**updated regularly**"
/ on a "**rolling basis**" over the **next 12 months**, with "substantially
more documents" as Law Department review and PII redaction complete, and
NYPD/DOHMH/EDC records "represented going forward." **No specific dates
have been published for any future tranche.** [NYC Mayor's Office](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/09/25-years-later--mamdani-administration-opens-city-s-9-11-records) ·
[amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/)

**The DOI inquiry:** the City Council mandated a **two-year** Department of
Investigation inquiry (2025) into the City's handling of post-9/11
air-quality information — the first use of this City Charter provision for
a Council-mandated DOI probe. DOI told the Council it needed **$4M** to
hire an outside firm to locate/review records; that funding was included in
the FY27 budget. Expected output: a report on what the City knew about
airborne contaminants and when. It runs in parallel to, and partly
triggered, the portal's document review (DEP's admission about the 68
boxes came out of this inquiry). [amNewYork](https://www.amny.com/news/9-11-public-portal-mamdani-releases-first-170000-pages-as-records-renew-questions-over-air-quality-warnings/) ·
[NYC Council](https://council.nyc.gov/press/2026/03/16/3088)

## 3. How it's being used and covered so far

**Specific document citations already published:** CBS New York, amNewYork,
NBC New York, CNN, the Inquirer, and The City Reporter have all quoted the
Harding Memo, Fuchs memo, the May 2002 "Clean Up Initiative" memo, and the
McKinney memo (§1 above) within days of the release — this is unusually
fast, document-driven coverage rather than press-release paraphrase.
[CBS NY](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/9-11-air-quality-documents-records-nyc/) ·
[The City Reporter](https://www.thecityreporter.nyc/2026/09/08/mamdani-9-11-air-quality-documents-giuliani-bloomberg-eric-adams/)

**Gothamist** has run a follow-on angle: the release could expand the
number of people eligible for federal WTC Health Program benefits, by
documenting exposure/timeline facts that affect eligibility determinations.
[Gothamist](https://gothamist.com/news/nycs-911-document-release-could-expand-number-of-people-receiving-federal-benefits)

**A third party is already building an independent tool against the
portal**: **`sept11documents-mcp`** by GitHub user **pranava0x0**
([repo](https://github.com/pranava0x0/sept11documents-mcp), described via
[Glama's MCP listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/pranava0x0/sept11documents-mcp)).
It's an MCP server, not a public search website — a local tool for an LLM
client. It ships four operations: `catalog_search` (search a **local
metadata snapshot** by Bates number/source/agency/box/folder),
`portal_search` (proxies full-text search to the live portal, gated behind
an explicit `SEPT11_ALLOW_LIVE=1` env flag), `portal_browse` (navigate the
local snapshot's source/box/folder hierarchy), and `portal_changes_since`
(diff two catalog snapshots to surface additions). It explicitly **does
not re-host documents** — it links to the City's copies — and it withholds
captured page text where its own PII screen trips. It states it is
independent, unaffiliated with the City or 9/11 Health Watch, and offers
"no legal advice." This is the closest thing found to a competing project;
it is metadata/citation-verification tooling for LLM use, not a public
browsable archive, so there's room alongside it rather than direct overlap
— but its catalog-snapshot + Bates/box/folder indexing approach is worth
studying before designing ours.

**No usability complaints about the portal itself** turned up in searches
(no threads, no reviews) as of 2026-09-13 — either too soon, or complaints
are happening in venues (Bluesky/X, Reddit) not well indexed yet by search.
Worth a follow-up pass in a week.

## 4. Legal/reuse posture

The records are City of New York government records, released under and in
settlement of **NY Freedom of Information Law (FOIL)** litigation (Article
78 proceedings, NYS Supreme Court) — they are public records, and nothing
found suggests the City claims copyright or imposes reuse restrictions
beyond ordinary site terms of use. The City states redaction is ongoing:
some material is withheld or redacted for **personally identifiable
information (PII)** and unspecified security-related content, and the
portal is being expanded over 12 months precisely because that PII review
is agency-by-agency and not finished. Coverage repeatedly notes PII removal
as the gating step for further releases, and the City has said it wants the
public to flag anything that slips through — but a direct fetch of the
portal's own PII-report-form language did not resolve (site returned no
usable content to an automated fetch); confirm the exact mechanism by
checking the live portal for a "report an issue" / PII-report link before
building anything that assumes its existence or wording. [NYC Mayor's Office](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/09/25-years-later--mamdani-administration-opens-city-s-9-11-records) ·
[911 Health Watch](https://www.911healthwatch.org/effort-to-get-new-york-city-to-release-9-11-documents/)

## 5. Suggested product angles

- **Timeline of who-knew-what-when**: sequence every named memo (Harding,
  Fuchs, Clean Up Initiative, McKinney) against the parallel public
  statements (Giuliani's Oct 6, 2001 "air is safe" declaration lands the
  *same day* as the McKinney memo warning otherwise) — the gap between
  internal and public language is the story, and no one has laid it out as
  a single scrollable timeline yet.
- **Agency/date/box facets**: the portal's own organizing unit is Bates
  number/source/agency/box/folder (per the pranava0x0 project's schema) —
  build real faceted browse on top of that rather than the "no document
  dates published" limitation others have hit.
- **Harding Memo's 35,000-plaintiff estimate vs. reality**: cross the 2001
  Law Department projection (~35,000 potential plaintiffs, ~10,000 expected
  claims) against actual WTC Health Program enrollment (CDC quarterly
  summaries) — a simple, sourceable, and startling comparison nobody has
  published yet.
- **Entity/official index**: every named official (Harding, Fuchs, Cardozo,
  Frieden, Shaw, Skyler, McKinney, Giuliani) cross-linked to every document
  that names them — officials acting in office only, never private
  individuals.
- **Internal-memo vs. press-release diff view**: pair each internal
  document with the corresponding public statement from the same week (a
  press release, a mayoral quote) side by side.
- **DEP "68 boxes" provenance thread**: document the discovery itself as a
  story — DOI-mandated inquiry → DEP's admission → 20,000+ documents found
  — since that chain is itself evidence of the City's original
  record-locating claims being wrong.
- **Sampling-location map**: DEP/EPA environmental readings (dust, air,
  asbestos) geocoded against Lower Manhattan, layered with school/residence
  locations — the Avalon EPA collection has structured daily summaries that
  could seed this even before the portal's own DEP data is fully mapped.
- **Release-tranche tracker**: a standing page tracking what's landed vs.
  promised (NYPD/DOHMH/EDC "going forward," the WTC Captive Insurance
  Company's unreleased trove, the 12-month settlement clock) so "promised
  but not delivered" is always visible and dated.
- **Cross-source reconciliation**: merge portal documents with the
  pre-existing bulk sources in §2 (Avalon EPA pages, NARA Commission MFRs,
  EPA OIG reports) so a single search surfaces both the newly released
  internal record and the contemporaneous public-facing federal record.
- **DOI-inquiry companion**: a page tracking the Council-mandated DOI
  two-year investigation's own findings as they're released, since it's
  running on a separate track from the portal and easy for readers to
  conflate.
