# NYC Council outreach list — 911records.nyc

Research date: Sept 14, 2026. Web research only; no emails sent, no code changed. All email addresses
below are copied verbatim from each member's own official page at council.nyc.gov (district-office or
legislative-office address as published) — none were guessed or pattern-constructed. Where only a
contact form or general inbox exists, that is stated rather than inferred. District boundaries and
committee chairs are current 2026-term assignments per council.nyc.gov.

**Story context for all pitches:** last week the Mayor released ~172,000 pages of the City's 9/11
records (DEP, FDNY, DDC, DOB, DCAS, Mayor's Office) through a single-search-box public portal. Founder
is a Brooklyn 9/11 family member whose father-in-law, Hughie Yee, died of a WTC-related cancer after a
hard fight to get VCF-certified; he built 911records.nyc, a free independent explorer: 3D map of lower
Manhattan, browsing by the City's own record boxes, plain-language Q&A with page citations, VCF case
folders and exhibit lists, and change tracking.

## Suggested outreach email

**To:** Speaker Menin's office (SpeakerMenin@council.nyc.gov, attn. Chief of Staff Miguelina Camilo) —
adapt the first two sentences for any other member using the per-row opening line above.

**Subject:** A free tool to search the City's 172,000-page 9/11 records, built by a 9/11 family

Hi Miguelina,

I'm the founder of 911records.nyc, a free, independent tool built after the City released roughly
172,000 pages of 9/11 records from DEP, FDNY, DDC, DOB, DCAS, and the Mayor's Office through its new
public portal. My father-in-law, Hughie Yee, died of a WTC-related cancer after a long fight to get
VCF-certified, and that experience shaped what we built: a 3D map of lower Manhattan, browsing by the
City's own record boxes, plain-language questions answered with page citations, ready-made case folders
and exhibit lists for VCF claims, and tracking of what changes as the City releases more.

Speaker Menin has pushed harder than almost anyone on the Council to get these records into the open, so
I wanted her office to see it directly rather than hear about it secondhand. I'm not asking for anything
beyond a look — no funding, no endorsement. I'd welcome the chance to give a short briefing to Council
staff and, especially, to member offices' caseworkers, since constituents can search the records by
their own home or work address to see what the City recorded about their building.

Happy to set up a call or a walkthrough whenever is convenient.

Thanks for your time,
[Founder name]
911records.nyc
