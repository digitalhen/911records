# LinkedIn launch post — 911records.nyc

## Main post (target 220–320 words)

Last week the Mayor's office released the City's 9/11 records: about 24,000 documents, 172,000+ pages, from the agencies that responded and cleaned up afterward — DEP, DCAS, FDNY, DORIS, DDC, and DOB. That's a real disclosure and it matters. But the portal the City built to hold it is a single search box. No browsing. No dates. No way to pull more than one document at a time. If you don't already know the right words to search for, you won't find what you need.

This is personal for me. My father-in-law died of a cancer the federal government recognizes as caused by 9/11 exposure. When our family tried to get him certified with the Victim Compensation Fund, we couldn't find the paperwork that proved where he'd been and what he'd been exposed to. The records existed somewhere in a City filing system. We just couldn't get to them.

So my company, Cleartext Labs, built 911records.nyc: an independent mirror and explorer of the same records, free, no account required. It has a 3D map of lower Manhattan that lights up every building named in the records. You can search with real filters, browse the collection the way the City itself organized it — box by box, folder by folder — or look up a lab, an agency, a contractor, a substance, an address, or an official. Ask a plain-language question and get an answer where every sentence cites the exact page it came from, or a table (try "which buildings were tested for asbestos"). Save pages to a case folder and export an exhibit list. We track what the City adds or removes over time.

911records.nyc isn't affiliated with the City. If you find something personal that should have been redacted, there's a link on every page to report it.

I hope this helps someone else get what they're owed.
